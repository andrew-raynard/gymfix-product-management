# GymFix — User Journey Mapping

**Project:** GymFix  
**Phase:** 2 — User Research  
**Date:** October 2026  
**Status:** Provisional Journey Maps

## 1. Objective

This document maps the potential end-to-end experience of reporting and resolving faulty gym equipment.

It compares a hypothetical current-state journey with the proposed GymFix future-state journey to identify friction points and product opportunities.

These journeys are informed by secondary research but have not been directly observed at a specific gym.

## 2. Current-State Journey: Gym Member

**Scenario:** A member discovers a faulty treadmill during a workout.

| Stage | User Action | Potential Pain Point | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discover | Notices equipment malfunction | Safety concerns and workout disruption | Clear equipment identification |
| 2. Locate staff | Searches for an employee | Reporting may interrupt workout | Accessible digital reporting |
| 3. Report | Explains the issue verbally | Information may be incomplete | Structured fault submission |
| 4. Wait | Continues workout or leaves | Limited repair visibility | Report acknowledgement |
| 5. Follow up | Asks about equipment later | Uncertain repair progress | Status tracking |
| 6. Resolution | Equipment becomes available | Member may not know it was repaired | Clear resolution status |

### Potential Moments of Friction

- Difficulty locating staff.
- Uncertainty about whether reports are recorded.
- Lack of repair status visibility.
- Repeated enquiries about equipment availability.

### Evidence Assessment

Public discussions support the existence of downtime and communication concerns.

However, the exact sequence of actions above is hypothetical and requires validation.

## 3. Current-State Journey: Gym Manager

**Scenario:** A staff member receives a report of faulty equipment.

| Stage | Staff Action | Potential Challenge | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Receive | Receives a fault report | Incomplete information | Standardised reporting |
| 2. Record | Documents the issue | Different reporting channels | Centralised ticket system |
| 3. Assess | Determines severity | Unclear equipment condition | Fault categorisation |
| 4. Assign | Contacts technician | Coordination overhead | Ticket assignment |
| 5. Monitor | Checks repair progress | Parts or technician delays | Blocker tracking |
| 6. Close | Confirms equipment is repaired | Inconsistent records | Resolution history |

These operational steps are workflow assumptions rather than observed processes.

## 4. Proposed Future-State Journey: Gym Member

| Stage | Proposed GymFix Experience | Expected Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Identify | Scan equipment QR code | Reduce equipment identification effort |
| 2. Report | Select fault category and add details | Improve report consistency |
| 3. Confirm | Receive ticket acknowledgement | Reduce uncertainty |
| 4. Track | View ticket status | Improve visibility |
| 5. Resolve | See equipment marked as repaired | Support workout planning |

These benefits are hypotheses to be evaluated through testing.

## 5. Proposed Future-State Journey: Gym Manager

| Stage | Proposed GymFix Experience | Expected Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Receive | View incoming reports in dashboard | Centralise requests |
| 2. Review | Inspect equipment and fault details | Improve information quality |
| 3. Assign | Assign maintenance responsibility | Clarify ownership |
| 4. Monitor | Track status and blockers | Improve coordination |
| 5. Close | Record repair completion | Maintain repair history |

## 6. Opportunity Areas

### Opportunity A — Reduce Reporting Friction

Potential feature: QR-based equipment reporting.

Hypothesis: Users can report faults more efficiently than through informal channels.

### Opportunity B — Improve Maintenance Visibility

Potential feature: Ticket acknowledgement and status tracking.

Hypothesis: Members and staff can more easily determine whether a fault is being addressed.

### Opportunity C — Improve Repair Coordination

Potential feature: Centralised dashboard with ticket assignment and blocker tracking.

Hypothesis: Staff can coordinate maintenance work more systematically.

## 7. Key Assumptions

- Members will be willing to scan QR codes.
- Staff will consistently update maintenance statuses.
- Technicians can access and update assigned tickets.
- Better reporting visibility will improve perceived service quality.
- Operators will find the system more valuable than existing alternatives.

## 8. Proposed Validation Activities

- Test whether members can submit a report without assistance.
- Test whether members can locate repair status.
- Test whether staff can assign and update tickets.
- Measure task completion time and success rate.
- Gather feedback about confusing steps and unnecessary features.

## 9. Conclusion

The journey mapping exercise identifies three potential opportunities: simpler reporting, clearer maintenance visibility, and improved repair coordination.

However, the current-state journeys remain hypothetical and should not be treated as observed customer behaviour.

These maps will inform the product strategy, MVP requirements, and subsequent usability testing.
