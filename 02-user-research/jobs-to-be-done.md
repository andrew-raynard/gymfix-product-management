# GymFix — Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)

**Project:** GymFix — Equipment Maintenance Management System  
**Phase:** 2 — User Research  
**Date:** October 2026  
**Research Basis:** Secondary research and provisional user personas  
**Status:** Draft — Requires Primary Validation

## 1. Objective

This document identifies the functional, emotional, and operational outcomes GymFix's potential users are trying to achieve.

Rather than beginning with specific product features, the JTBD framework helps clarify user motivations and determine which problems a future MVP should address.

The statements below are candidate jobs informed by secondary research and workflow assumptions. They have not been validated through direct user interviews.

## 2. Primary User: Gym Member

### JTBD 1 — Report Faulty Equipment

**When** I encounter equipment that appears faulty during my workout,  
**I want to** notify the responsible staff quickly,  
**So I can** avoid unsafe equipment and continue my workout with minimal disruption.

Functional outcome:
- Communicate a fault with minimal effort.
- Identify the affected equipment accurately.

Emotional outcome:
- Feel confident that the concern has been received.

Potential success measures:
- Time required to submit a fault report.
- Percentage of users who complete the reporting flow.
- Percentage of reports with sufficient equipment information.

### JTBD 2 — Understand Equipment Availability

**When** equipment I want to use is unavailable,  
**I want to** understand whether it is being repaired,  
**So I can** adjust my workout plans without repeatedly asking staff.

Functional outcome:
- Check equipment status.
- Understand whether a repair is pending or completed.

Emotional outcome:
- Reduce uncertainty and frustration.

Potential success measures:
- Percentage of users able to locate repair status.
- Time required to find equipment status.

## 3. Primary Operational User: Gym Manager

### JTBD 3 — Coordinate Maintenance

**When** an equipment fault is reported,  
**I want to** record, assign, and track the issue,  
**So I can** ensure that maintenance requests are handled systematically.

Functional outcome:
- Maintain a centralised record of unresolved issues.
- Assign responsibility.
- Monitor ticket progress.

Potential success measures:
- Time from report submission to assignment.
- Percentage of tickets with an assigned owner.
- Number of unresolved tickets.

### JTBD 4 — Identify Repair Blockers

**When** a repair is delayed,  
**I want to** understand the cause and required next action,  
**So I can** coordinate resources and communicate progress.

Functional outcome:
- Identify tickets awaiting parts, technicians, or approval.
- Track how long issues remain unresolved.

Potential success measures:
- Percentage of delayed tickets with a recorded blocker.
- Average ticket resolution time.

## 4. Supporting User: Maintenance Technician

### JTBD 5 — Receive Actionable Work Orders

**When** I am assigned a maintenance task,  
**I want to** access accurate equipment information and fault details,  
**So I can** diagnose the issue without unnecessary back-and-forth.

Functional outcome:
- Identify equipment and location.
- Review fault descriptions.
- Update repair progress.

Potential success measures:
- Percentage of tickets with complete fault details.
- Time required to review a work order.

## 5. JTBD Prioritisation Hypotheses

| JTBD | User | Initial Priority | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report faulty equipment | Member | High | Entry point for fault reporting |
| Understand equipment status | Member | High | Potential communication gap |
| Coordinate maintenance | Manager | High | Core operational workflow |
| Identify repair blockers | Manager | Medium | Relevant to prolonged repairs |
| Receive actionable work orders | Technician | Medium | Supports repair execution |

These priorities are preliminary and will be evaluated using a formal prioritisation framework in Phase 4.

## 6. Research Evidence and Limitations

Phase 1 identified public accounts involving prolonged equipment downtime, uncertain repair progress, and existing low-cost reporting methods.

However:
- Reporting friction has limited direct evidence.
- The proposed workflows have not been observed end-to-end.
- Technician needs have not been directly validated.
- Willingness to adopt or pay for GymFix remains unknown.

Refer to the Phase 1 discovery report and research evidence spreadsheet for the underlying sources.

## 7. Implications for Product Development

The strongest preliminary product hypothesis is that GymFix should support a complete maintenance workflow rather than only fault submission.

Potential capabilities include:
- Equipment-specific reporting.
- Ticket acknowledgement.
- Staff assignment.
- Maintenance status tracking.
- Repair blocker documentation.

No feature is considered validated solely because it appears in this document.
