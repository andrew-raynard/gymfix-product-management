# GymFix — Key Learnings & Product Reflection

**Phase:** 8 — Product Roadmap & Learnings  
**Project Type:** Independent Product Management Case Study

## 1. Project Reflection

GymFix was developed to explore how a digital product could improve gym equipment fault reporting, maintenance coordination, and repair visibility.

The project followed a structured Product Management process, from secondary research and problem discovery through strategy, prioritisation, requirements, prototyping, and evaluation planning.

## 2. Key Learning — Problem Discovery

The initial product idea focused on making it easier for members to report broken equipment.

Secondary research suggested that maintenance coordination and repair visibility could also be important.

This demonstrated the value of exploring the underlying problem before committing to a specific solution.

## 3. Key Learning — Product Prioritisation

The RICE framework helped compare candidate features using estimated reach, impact, confidence, and effort.

However, the scores were based on assumptions rather than production usage data.

The exercise demonstrated that quantitative frameworks support product judgement but do not replace evidence or consideration of workflow dependencies.

## 4. Key Learning — MVP Scope

A focused MVP can demonstrate a complete user journey without implementing every possible feature.

GymFix prioritised fault reporting, ticket management, technician assignment, and maintenance status tracking.

More complex capabilities were deferred to avoid unnecessary scope expansion.

## 5. Key Learning — Prototyping and Iteration

The initial Lovable implementation contained navigation issues, including missing pages.

After addressing these issues, seven manual functional checks passed.

This reinforced the importance of verifying functional behaviour rather than evaluating a prototype solely by its appearance.

## 6. Key Learning — Measuring Product Success

The project established proposed KPIs covering reporting efficiency, task success, maintenance accountability, and resolution tracking.

However, real-world performance has not been measured.

Phase 7 used a clearly labelled simulated usability exercise rather than actual participant testing.

This distinction is essential for interpreting the project's findings responsibly.

## 7. What I Would Do Differently

If continuing GymFix, I would:

1. Conduct direct interviews with gym operators earlier.
2. Compare existing maintenance workflows in more detail.
3. Establish stronger evidence for the proposed target segment.
4. Test usability with actual participants.
5. Validate commercial demand before expanding development.

## 8. Skills Demonstrated

- Problem discovery and secondary research.
- Evidence-informed user modelling.
- Product strategy and value proposition development.
- Feature prioritisation using RICE.
- PRD and acceptance criteria writing.
- MVP scope management.
- AI-assisted prototyping.
- Manual functional validation.
- KPI design and product roadmap planning.

## 9. Final Reflection

The most important lesson from GymFix is that Product Management involves making informed decisions under uncertainty.

Building a working prototype is one milestone. Establishing customer value, usability, and business viability requires additional evidence.

GymFix demonstrates an end-to-end product planning and prototyping process, while clearly identifying the research and validation still needed.
